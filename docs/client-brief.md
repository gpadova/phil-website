## What the product actually is

Properly structured, properly designed, max-funded, tax-advantaged **Indexed Universal Life (IUL)** policies.

His framing: these are sophisticated financial asset-class instruments used for business or personal purposes while you're alive — the death benefit "comes along for the ride." Structured correctly under specific parts of the tax code, the cost of insurance goes *down* over time rather than up.

## The three objections he has to break

This is a good landing-page spine.

1. "I have assets, so I don't need life insurance."
2. "It gets more expensive every year, and I have better uses for my money with better returns."
3. "It's illiquid — once my money's in, I can't get it out."

**His answer to all three:** that's only true because most people don't know how to structure and build them correctly.

## Benefit pillars

### Tax treatment
Grows tax-deferred like an IRA or Roth — without the contribution caps or income phase-outs. His contrast: ~$7,600/yr max in an IRA, ~$26,000/yr in a 401(k), locked until 59½, 10% penalty for touching your own money, and then you're taxed on a balance that's several times larger.

### Seed vs. harvest
Pay the tax on the seed, not on the forest. Traditional deferral means you skipped a small tax bill decades ago and now pay it on 4x the money, when everything also costs 4x more.

### Liquidity
Borrow against the cash value tax-free at ~5%, with no obligation to repay. At death, the loan and accrued interest are settled out of the death benefit and the remainder goes to beneficiaries — which is why it never has to be repaid.

### Be your own bank / positive arbitrage
When you borrow, the accumulation column keeps growing at the full rate — it isn't drawn down. Borrow at 5%, earn 10–15% inside, or deploy the money outside into real estate, equities, whatever, and keep both engines running.

### Downside protection
When markets fall, the value doesn't fall — it holds and restarts from there. Negative years are eliminated. His 2008 illustration: $100K drops to $65K at -35%; a 20% recovery year gets you to $78K, still not even, while the protected account would be at $120K.

### Flexibility
After the initial max-funding window you never have to contribute another dollar. Lean year? Put in $10K. Good year later? Catch up, and after a set number of years you can resume larger contributions.

### Conservative math
His illustrations run at 6.59% while long-term market averages are ~8–11%.

## Proof / case-study material

> ⚠️ See [Compliance flag](#compliance-flag) before putting any of these figures on a public page.

**69-year-old real estate investor**
$1M funded over 6 years (as fast as the tax code allows), $1.4M initial death benefit, ~$969K cash value by year 6 at 6.59%. Borrowed $250K tax-free, bought Micron around $150/share (now ~$997) — roughly $1.6M. By his 90s: $1.4M in total, $250K taken out, and beneficiaries receive nearly $4M tax-free.

**42-year-old single dad / business owner**
$300K funded at ~$74K/yr over 4.5 years, buying $1M of protection so his son's education and start in life are covered regardless.

**The cost argument**
At year 15, cost of insurance $913 plus $120 admin — roughly $1,033 — against $31,000 of credited interest. "If you hired an employee for $1,000, would you fire them for making you $31,000?" $1M of coverage at age 57 for about $900.

**Fee comparison**
He's paid once, by the carrier, at placement. A brokerage account charges ~1% every year forever — by age 89 that single year's fee is ~$28,000.
